# Supplement for players under 18: Chapters 3 to 5

Used by the multi-step rollout ([SWED-100](https://app.plane.so/the-equal-lens/projects/59d0b01f-352e-4aee-bd3f-252cdf283a74/issues/8449d339-a540-489c-88e3-61d3d670fdd4))
for games whose players are children and teenagers (Chapter 3, ages 9 to 12; Chapter 4, ages 12 to 15; Chapter 5, ages
15 to 18). Writers, reviewers and fixers apply it on top of `steps-write.md` and the game's GROUNDING.md, and it wins
where they differ. It follows The Equal Lens child-safe content rules.

## The rules

- **A story, never a question about the player's own life.** No option, `then`, `why` or prompt asks the player what
  has happened to them, who hurt them, or whether something is happening at home. The scenario is a moment in a
  story, and the player chooses what the character does.
- **When a young person is harmed, pressured or unsafe** (by an adult, an older teen, a partner, a group, or online),
  the best move across the story includes telling a trusted adult: a parent or carer, a teacher, a school counsellor.
  Where the source or GROUNDING.md names a helpline, keep it exactly as written there (Childline 1098; 112 in
  danger). The same survival rules as for adults apply: freezing, going along, keeping quiet out of fear or not
  telling straight away are never options marked short of best, no `then` shows harm happening because of the pick,
  and a `why` or the `debrief` says it is never their fault.
- **Unsafe secrets.** A secret an adult asks a child to keep, or one that makes them feel scared or uneasy, is an
  unsafe secret, and telling is always right. Never offer "keep it between us" as anything but a tempting wrong
  option, and never have the best move be confronting an adult who harms alone.
- **Helping a friend.** The best move believes the friend, stays with them, and gets a trusted adult involved. It is
  never a promise to keep the friend's secret when the friend is at risk, and never a risky rescue or a fight.
- **Refer, don't treat.** Contraception, infections, pregnancy, mental-health treatment and medicines are referred to
  a doctor, clinic or counsellor with a trusted adult. No option gives clinical advice, and no `then` diagnoses.
- **Right for the age.** Ages 9 to 12: bodies, puberty, feelings, friendships, safe and unsafe touch, online safety; no
  sexual activity. Ages 12 to 15: crushes, consent basics, respect in friendships and first relationships, online
  pressure and sharing images. Ages 15 to 18: dating, consent and its limits, sexual health referrals, rights, stated
  plainly without law names or section numbers.
- **Words.** "Safe touch" and "unsafe touch", never "good touch" and "bad touch". Body parts by their proper names,
  plainly, where the band covers them. No feeling is bad; some are hard. Bodies, puberty and difference are normal,
  never a joke or a shame.
- **Tone.** Warm and plain. No shaming, no scare stories, nothing graphic. A wrong option is a real mistake a young
  person might make, never a cartoon.

## For reviewers

Treat any break of these rules as a `block` finding under the `safety` lens.
