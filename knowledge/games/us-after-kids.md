**Node #g61: Chapter 8, Parenthood (first child on).** *A baby changes everything, including the two of you.*
Chapter 8 begins not with the baby but with the **relationship and the self that a new baby strains hardest**:
the [Choosing & Building](choosing-building.md) partnership (g53) now under the load of sleeplessness and a third
person who needs everything. Lensy returns as a been-there peer. **The spine: the post-baby dip is normal and not a
verdict; you fight the tiredness, not each other; you share the load so it doesn't curdle into resentment; you
reconnect on your own clock; and you both stay whole people, with fathers as equal parents, never "helpers."**

## What it embodies

- **The big shift (14)**: a baby reshapes the couple and each self; **~2 in 3 couples feel the dip**: it's
  normal, not a verdict, and the thrivers use learnable skills.
- **Talk through the tired (14)**: communication and repair under exhaustion; name the tiredness, not each other;
  appreciation; assume good intent; busts *"the romance is just over."*
- **Share don't resent (14)**: fair division is the strongest relationship protector; **fathers are engaged
  directly**: a father isn't "babysitting" his own child (links Equal Parents g62).
- **Reconnecting (14)**: rebuild intimacy at *both* partners' pace, **no deadline, no obligation**; recovery is
  individual; postpartum discomfort → a clinician.
- **You still matter (14)**: protect each partner's identity and wellbeing; you're a whole person, not only a
  parent; persistent low mood → Looking After You (g63).
- **Tools & help (14)**: a daily check-in and reconnect toolkit, protected couple time even in a joint family,
  and the help routes.

**Stance & safeguarding.** Even-handed and warm: fathers as equal parents, partnership not pity, **no pressure on
intimacy timing**, frank but respectful, and **not medical advice**. Persistent low mood is named as **perinatal
depression** (real, common, treatable, not a failing) and routed to a clinician / Tele-MANAS 14416; strain that
turns to control or abuse routes to Respect at Home (g56) and help. India: a new baby often arrives within a joint
family (help, but also pressure and less couple privacy), and postpartum women face a dual burden, role conflict
and guilt.
